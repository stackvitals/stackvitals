Flaky tests, stuck reviews, slow releases and risky updates, in one place.

StackVitals is a free GitHub Action. Every few hours it reads your CI runs, pull requests and releases, keeps the history in a branch of your own repo, and shows what needs a decision. No hosted backend, no account, nothing to host.

From GitHub to your app

GitHub

Your CI runs, pull requests, issues and deployments.

Your workflow

After every CI run and every 3 hours, it reads with the workflow's own token, about a minute a run.

stackvitals-data

A branch in your repo: every number with its history, every decision with who and why.

Your app

A static page on your GitHub Pages, or on your machine for private repos.

Decisions happen where you already work: type /stackvitals quarantine in any issue or pull request, or press Decide in the app. Every decision is logged with who made it, why and until when.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. It runs in your own GitHub Actions minutes (about a minute a run) and keeps everything in your repo. There is no StackVitals service to pay for or sign up to.

Does it work for private repos?

Yes. The workflow is the same. GitHub Pages sites are public, so a private repo's app opens on your machine with stackvitals ui owner/repo, or in the app here with a token that can read the repo.

Where does my data go?

Into a branch called stackvitals-data in your own repo, and nowhere else, unless you add a chat webhook yourself.

What can it change in my repo?

Only what someone with access asks for: a /stackvitals command or a request from the app is checked against that person's GitHub access before anything is recorded or changed.

Does it replace the paid tools?

It covers the core workflows of the tools in the table for GitHub-first teams. It doesn't do investment allocation, surveys, hosted runners or on-call.

How do I remove it?

Delete the workflow file, delete the stackvitals-data branch and turn GitHub Pages off. Nothing is left anywhere else.
